Residente ka ng PH at kumikita? **Kadalasan may obligasyon ka sa BIR**, kahit foreign client ang nagbabayad. General overview lang ito, hindi tax advice.

## ✅ Bakit mag-register

:::cards
⚖️ | Legal at panatag | Iwas sa penalties
📄 | Proof of income | Loan, credit card, visa, upa
💼 | Mas professional | May clients na humihingi ng invoice
:::

## 🪜 Basic na registration flow

:::steps
🔢 | Kunin o i-update ang TIN, huwag kumuha ng bago
📝 | Mag-register bilang self-employed o professional
⚖️ | Piliin ang tax option: 8% o graduated
📜 | Kunin ang Certificate of Registration
🧾 | Ayusin ang invoices at books of accounts
📅 | Alamin ang quarterly at annual filing schedule
:::

## 💯 Ang 8% option sa isang tingin

:::stats
8% | Income tax sa gross receipts o sales
₱250k | Ibinabawas sa annual gross, para sa purely self-employed
0 | Hiwalay na percentage tax, kapalit na ng 8%
:::

## ⚖️ Graduated rates vs 8%

:::vs
📉 Graduated rates | 💯 8% option
Basehan: net taxable income | Basehan: gross receipts
Unang ₱250k nasa 0% bracket | ₱250k ibinabawas sa gross
May hiwalay na percentage tax | Kapalit na ng percentage tax
Mas detalyadong records | Mas simple sa karamihan
Bagay kung malaki ang expenses | Bagay kung maliit ang expenses
:::

:::tip
🧮 Halimbawa lang: ₱600k gross, bawas ₱250k = ₱350k. 8% nito = ₱28,000.
:::

## 📒 Record keeping

:::check
I-save lahat ng invoices sa client
I-download ang history sa Wise, PayPal, Payoneer, o bank
Monthly spreadsheet: date, client, USD, PHP, fees
I-save ang resibo ng work expenses
Mag-set aside ng porsyento ng bawat sweldo para sa tax
:::

:::tip
🏛️ May thresholds at deadlines. I-verify sa bir.gov.ph, sa RDO mo, o sa accountant bago mag-file.
:::
